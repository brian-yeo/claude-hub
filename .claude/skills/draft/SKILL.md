# Draft

## Standing rules

- **Read `personal/voice.md` before writing a word.** If it doesn't exist, say so once, offer BUILD VOICE, and write in a plain direct register rather than inventing a personality.
- **Never change what a sentence means to make it shorter.** If a cut would, keep it and flag the bloat instead.
- **Real uncertainty stays hedged.** False confidence is a worse sin than a wasted word.

## Pick the mode

| They gave you | Mode |
|---|---|
| An intent, an audience, some points | **DRAFT** |
| Existing text that's too long or too soft | **TIGHTEN** |
| Samples of their own writing | **BUILD VOICE** |

---

## DRAFT

Settle these four first. Ask only for what you genuinely can't infer, in one round — not an interrogation.

1. **Audience** — who reads this, what do they already know?
2. **The ask** — what should they do or decide? If nothing, is this needed at all?
3. **Register** — where between a Slack message and a signed document?
4. **Constraint** — length, format, anything that must or must not appear.

Then:

- **Lead with the point.** First sentence carries the news or the ask. Context after, and only what's load-bearing.
- **One idea per paragraph.** If it needs "also", it's two paragraphs.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Significant improvement" says nothing; "cut p99 from 1.8s to 400ms" says it.
- **One explicit ask.** Who does what by when. Three asks usually gets zero done.
- **Close with the next step**, not a pleasantry.

Deliver the draft, then two lines on the choices the user might want to reverse.

---

## TIGHTEN

Most drafts lose 30–50% with no loss of meaning and read stronger for it.

**Cut on sight:**

- Throat-clearing — "I wanted to reach out about", "Just circling back", "I hope this finds you well", "As you may know"
- Stacked hedges — "I think it might possibly be worth maybe considering"
- Meta-commentary — "In this section we will discuss", "It's worth noting that"
- Nominalizations — "make a decision" → "decide"; "provide assistance" → "help"
- Adverbs propping up weak verbs — "very quickly" → "fast"; "really important" → name the stake
- Passive voice hiding the actor, unless the actor genuinely doesn't matter
- Any sentence restating the previous one in different words

**Do not cut:**

- Genuine uncertainty
- The specifics — "reduced latency 4x on the checkout path" → "improved performance" is deletion, not tightening
- Warmth that's doing a job. A hard message with every softener stripped reads as a threat.

**Output:**

1. The tightened text
2. `<original> → <new> words`
3. What you cut and why, grouped by reason, not line by line
4. Anything left alone that looks cuttable but isn't, with the reason
5. For the two or three highest-stakes sentences, an alternative to choose from

---

## BUILD VOICE

From 3–5 real samples, extract a profile and write `personal/voice.md`.

Look for what's *characteristic*, not merely present:

- Sentence length and rhythm — uniform, or varied with short punches?
- Habitual openings and closings
- Formality, and whether it shifts by audience
- Contractions, first person, questions to the reader, humor
- Punctuation habits — em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks, lists
- Words reached for repeatedly; words conspicuously never used
- How they deliver bad news, disagree, and say no

**Quote real examples under every observation.** A profile of adjectives is useless; a profile with three of their actual sentences under each point is not.

Write it, show it, and ask what's wrong with it. People recognize their own voice better than they can describe it, so treat the first pass as a draft to react to.
